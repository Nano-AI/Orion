## Source-supported operational finding

**P3 — the motion page retains a frame cadence while idle.** ScrollTrigger registration starts a self-rescheduling `requestAnimationFrame` loop (`ScrollTrigger.js:10`, byte 34,902) and a 250 ms interval (`:10`, byte 36,003). Independently, Orion adds Lenis's `raf` callback to GSAP's ticker (`motion.js:30`); GSAP's auto-sleep condition requires fewer than two ticker listeners (`gsap.js:10`, byte 31,273), while its root update and Lenis callbacks are both registered (`gsap.js:10`, byte 52,378; `motion.js:30`). Orion adds a third callback for the sky (`motion.js:365`), which returns before drawing when sky visibility is near zero (`:368-370`) but still runs each tick. Consequently the active page continues scheduling JavaScript frames even at a still hero or after a scroll settles. This is an **energy/CPU measurement candidate**, not a measured slowdown or a claim that the vendor is defective. The default reduced-motion and `?nomotion` exits avoid the ScrollTrigger/Lenis registrations, and browser background-tab throttling may reduce actual work. Lenis's own `autoRaf` defaults false (`lenis.js:11`, byte 6,649), so its internal loop is not separately started by Orion; `motion.js` supplies the clock. The smallest follow-up is an idle, foreground browser performance capture on the landing hero and at a sky-visible section, compared with `?nomotion`, recording frames, CPU and energy before deciding whether any cadence change is worthwhile.

No other independent Orion-reachable defect was established. Lenis's input interception, anchor handling, resize observers and teardown (`lenis.js:11`, bytes 4,288–19,952) are normal conditional paths for the configured instance; the site's finite static reveal elements bound the `ScrollTrigger.batch` work. This audit did not execute an anchor click, resize, route change or animation, and cannot assert their rendered behavior.
